# Backbox v0.12 — keyed rear service door

Status: engineering provisional; not manufacturing-ready.

## Clarification

The backbox is **closed in normal use**. It is not an open-backed monitor rack.

The rear is a fixed structural frame with a deliberately sized service aperture. That aperture is closed by a gasketed, keyed door. The door exists so cables, fans, lighting, controllers and toy hardware can be serviced without removing the backglass monitor or dismantling the front of the backbox.

The service door is **not** the primary structural shear member. Rigidity comes from the 18 mm backbox perimeter, dedicated top and bottom crossmembers, fixed rear framing around the aperture, and the internal monitor-rail subframe tied into those fixed members.

## Service door concept

Provisional clear service opening: approximately **520 x 460 mm**.

Door requirements:

- plywood panel, minimum 12 mm; MDF is not accepted;
- full-height piano/continuous hinge or equivalent heavy-duty multi-hinge arrangement;
- keyed cam/quarter-turn lock as the baseline;
- two-point/compression locking may be adopted if prototype testing shows a large door needs additional anti-rattle or anti-warp clamping;
- closed-cell gasket around the full perimeter;
- no hand-openable latch during normal use;
- warning label on the inner face;
- no exposed live mains parts when the door is opened.

The two 120 mm backbox exhaust fans stay on a **fixed upper rear panel**, not on the service door. This avoids moving fan wiring every time the door is opened and preserves predictable airflow/filter geometry.

## What the rear door should let us service

The keyed rear door should provide practical access to:

- backglass HDMI/video and power leads;
- DMD cables;
- monitor-carriage locking/adjustment hardware;
- LED strips/controllers and lighting harnesses;
- low-voltage toy wiring/modules;
- fan controller and fan wiring;
- replaceable fan/filter hardware;
- cable strain-relief points and quick-disconnects.

A complete monitor swap is still preferably performed from the front because the display and bezel are designed as a front-removable module, but the rear door should make cable troubleshooting and mount adjustment possible without removing the display first.

## Structural strategy around the door

The aperture is surrounded by a fixed rear structural frame. Current provisional rule:

- minimum fixed rear-frame width around the opening: **90 mm** where geometry allows;
- fixed upper fan panel;
- dedicated top and bottom crossmembers;
- monitor T-slot rails anchored into fixed crossmembers/perimeter structure, never into the door alone;
- door may improve local stiffness when closed, but the backbox must remain structurally sound with the door open during service.

This is important because the backbox also folds through roughly 90 degrees for transport. The monitor/DMD loads and hinge loads must not depend on a removable/hinged service panel.

## Child / pest / shock protection

The door uses a key because service convenience must not make the cabinet easy for children to enter.

The enclosure rules remain:

- finger-safe fan guards;
- fine removable insect mesh/filter behind ventilation openings;
- gasketed rear door;
- gasketed backbox-floor/rear-shelf seam;
- compression/gland-style cable passports rather than open holes;
- unused reserve passport blanked and gasketed;
- no exposed mains screw terminals;
- any unavoidable mains splice stays inside a separate tool-access-only enclosure that remains closed even with the service door open.

The cabinet is intended to be an appliance/toy, not an electrical trap. Any design stage that introduces accessible mains potential is a design failure until corrected.

## Brazil-availability direction

The rear-door concept deliberately uses hardware classes readily obtainable in Brazil:

- cabinet/panel keyed cam locks ("fechadura lingueta" / panel locks);
- continuous/piano hinges in zinc-plated steel or stainless steel;
- EPDM/closed-cell adhesive gasket strip;
- aluminium/fiberglass insect mesh;
- ordinary metric machine screws, washers and threaded inserts/captive nuts.

Specific part numbers remain BOM-stage choices, but the geometry should avoid dependence on a single imported lock or hinge.
